Question

A polynomial, \(p(x)\), is given in factored form. \[p(x)~=~(x+8)\cdot (x+2)^{2}\cdot (x-5)\]

Which of the following graphs matches \(y=p(x)\)?


Answerlist

  • A
  • B
  • C
  • D

Solution

First, consider the end behavior. If you expanded the polynomial into standard form, the highest-degree term would be \(x^{4}\).

\[p(x)~=~x^{4}+\ldots\]

The highest degree term has a positive coefficient and an even degree, so the end behavior is “up,up”. Or, more formally, \(\lim\limits_{x\to-\infty} p(x) ~=~ \infty\) and \(\lim\limits_{x\to\infty} p(x) ~=~ \infty\).

Next, we consider the roots. From the factored form, \(p(x)~=~(x+8)\cdot (x+2)^{2}\cdot (x-5)\), it is easy to see (see zero-product property) that \(x=-8\) or \(x=-2\) or \(x=5\) makes the polynomial output 0.

\[p(-8)~=~(-8+8)\cdot (-8+2)^{2}\cdot (-8-5)~=~0\] \[p(-2)~=~(-2+8)\cdot (-2+2)^{2}\cdot (-2-5)~=~0\] \[p(5)~=~(5+8)\cdot (5+2)^{2}\cdot (5-5)~=~0\]

We predict that at \(x=-2\) there is a double root (multiplicity of 2) because the corresponding factor is squared. Graphically, we expect the curve to “bounce” off the \(x\) axis at \(x=-2\). At the other roots, we expect the curve to cut through the \(x\) axis.

Anyway, based on this analysis, we can produce a sign graph of the polynomial.

\(x\) interval or value sign analysis of \(p(x)~=~(x+8)\cdot (x+2)^{2}\cdot (x-5)\) sign
\((-\infty,-8)\) \((\mathrm{neg})\cdot (\mathrm{neg})^{2}\cdot (\mathrm{neg})\) \(1\)
\(-8\) \((\mathrm{zero})\cdot (\mathrm{neg})^{2}\cdot (\mathrm{neg})\) \(0\)
\((-8,-2)\) \((\mathrm{pos})\cdot (\mathrm{neg})^{2}\cdot (\mathrm{neg})\) \(-1\)
\(-2\) \((\mathrm{pos})\cdot (\mathrm{zero})^{2}\cdot (\mathrm{neg})\) \(0\)
\((-2,5)\) \((\mathrm{pos})\cdot (\mathrm{pos})^{2}\cdot (\mathrm{neg})\) \(-1\)
\(5\) \((\mathrm{pos})\cdot (\mathrm{pos})^{2}\cdot (\mathrm{zero})\) \(0\)
\((5,\infty)\) \((\mathrm{pos})\cdot (\mathrm{pos})^{2}\cdot (\mathrm{pos})\) \(1\)


And only one option matches.
