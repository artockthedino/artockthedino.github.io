Question

A video-game developer is hoping to rotate a set of points around the origin by 1.15 radians counterclockwise by using matrix multiplication.


The starting points are stored as rows in a 10 \(\times\) 2 matrix \(S\).

\[S=\begin{bmatrix} -9.499 & 1.13\\ -8.515 & 4.138\\ 1.75 & -4.451\\ 4.378 & -5.154\\ -6.285 & 0.05\\ 7.527 & -3.485\\ -2.874 & -9.27\\ -2.043 & 9.258\\ -7.056 & 6.065\\ -3.922 & -4.16 \end{bmatrix}\]

The 2D-rotation matrix can be found by using \(\theta=1.15\) in the formula below.

\[R~=~\begin{bmatrix}\cos(\theta)&\sin(\theta)\\ -\sin(\theta) & \cos(\theta)\end{bmatrix}\]

The final points (in matrix \(F\)) are found by multiplying \(S\cdot R\).

\[F=S\cdot R\]

That multiplication can be done using the diagram below.


As denoted by the question mark in the diagram above, when the point \((-3.922,-4.16)\) is rotated by 1.15 radians, what is the new \(y\) coordinate? The tolerance is \(\pm 0.001\).

Solution

First, you can get decimal approximations for the rotation-matrix elements by using \(\theta=1.15\).

\[R=\begin{bmatrix} 0.408487440884157 & 0.912763940260521\\ -0.912763940260521 & 0.408487440884157 \end{bmatrix}\]

The initial point \((-3.922,-4.16)\) is in row 10 of \(S\). Because we want to find the new \(y\) coordinate, use column 2 of \(R\).

\[(-3.922)(0.9127639)~+~(-4.16)(0.4084874)\]

So, the new \(y\) coordinate is -5.2791679.
